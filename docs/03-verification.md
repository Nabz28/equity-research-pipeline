# Verification

The checklist. Run it on every draft, before the narrative is polished, because polish
makes you reluctant to delete.

## Numbers

- [ ] every number has a named source and a date
- [ ] every number was read from a primary filing, the company's own deck, or the price
      series, not from a news summary of one
- [ ] percentages recomputed from the raw figures, not copied
- [ ] periods match: same fiscal quarter, same currency, same consolidation
- [ ] growth rates checked against the prior year's filing, not the prior year's press release
- [ ] one-offs separated from operating earnings and labelled
- [ ] peer multiples all from the same screener on the same day, with the day stated

## Prices and levels

- [ ] last price from the chart platform with the timeframe set explicitly
- [ ] returns computed from the bar series, not quoted from an article
- [ ] the 52 week high and low from the bars
- [ ] every level on the chart exists in the fact sheet, and every level in the fact sheet
      that is on the chart is drawn at the same price

## Claims

- [ ] each claim numbered and checked individually
- [ ] counts ("x quarters in a row", "x of the last y") recounted from the table
- [ ] attributions checked: who actually said it, in what document
- [ ] anything from a rumour or a chat screenshot marked as reported, not as fact
- [ ] "our view" separated from "reported", visually, on the slide itself

## The call

- [ ] a direction, LONG, SHORT or NEUTRAL, and NEUTRAL is a real answer
- [ ] a horizon in days, weeks or months
- [ ] an entry level or zone
- [ ] a stop, and the reason it is where it is
- [ ] a target, and the arithmetic that gets there
- [ ] risk and reward stated as a ratio
- [ ] what would prove it wrong, at least two triggers, one in each direction

## Delivery

- [ ] every slide rendered to an image and inspected
- [ ] no wrapped titles, no clipped text, no empty bottom third
- [ ] every PDF page measured for overflow, not eyeballed
- [ ] voice linter passes
- [ ] read out loud
